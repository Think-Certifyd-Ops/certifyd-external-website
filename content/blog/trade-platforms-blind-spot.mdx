A review score or company profile helps with one part of choosing a service. When somebody arrives, the household or receiving business still needs a practical way to confirm the expected appointment and worker.

## Read what the booking evidence covers

Check the provider's current terms and verification explanation. A platform may verify a business, account holder or particular attribute without checking every person sent to each job. Do not assume all platforms have identical capabilities or that a badge guarantees the arriving individual's suitability.

Keep the booking reference and contact route you established when arranging the work. If a substitute arrives, confirm the change through that route rather than a new phone number offered at the door.

## Separate authorisation from other checks

The person can be expected for an appointment while qualifications or access still require review. For a business receiving work, identify the relevant contract and role requirements. Use the [employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) where the specified working arrangement falls within its scope.

Do not apply business right-to-work responsibility to every household service purchase. The current guidance explains relevant categories and exclusions, including services for an individual's own use.

## Plan what happens when details differ

Ask the service provider to explain the changed worker or appointment. Keep access limited until you are satisfied through the agreed process. Avoid using a document displayed on an unfamiliar phone as the sole proof of authorisation.

Certifyd's current workforce scope is [worker records and attendance](/platform/records/). A shared record can support an authorised business handoff; it is not a universal consumer verification network, trade certification or guarantee about a person at the door.
