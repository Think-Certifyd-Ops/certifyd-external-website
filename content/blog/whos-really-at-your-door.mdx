The most useful doorstep question is whether this person is authorised for the appointment you arranged. A company logo, confident explanation or image of an identity document does not answer it by itself.

## Check the appointment independently

Use the established contact route from the original booking, or find the organisation's official details independently. Ask it to confirm the person, job and any change of worker. Avoid using only a phone number or link supplied by the visitor.

If the details do not match, keep access limited while you resolve the difference. Do not feel obliged to hand over personal information or make an immediate payment to complete the check.

## Understand what a badge can establish

A platform or service-provider badge has a stated scope. It may concern an account, business or attribute rather than each worker arriving for each visit. Read the provider's current explanation instead of assuming universal verification.

An attendance QR code records an interaction within a process. It does not itself prove a person's qualifications, criminal-record suitability, work permission or right to enter a household.

## Prepare for changed workers

Ask the provider how legitimate substitutions are communicated. Keep the booking reference and appropriate escalation contact available. For someone needing support, agree a simple [doorstep confirmation plan](/blog/vulnerable-adults-identity-doorstep/) beforehand.

Unexpected digital confirmations also require care. Follow the [NCSC's suspicious-message guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/spot-scams) and use an independent route to the organisation.
