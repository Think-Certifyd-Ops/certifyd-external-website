A worker can move between sites while their evidence remains in one manager's inbox. The next site needs the relevant decision and restrictions, with a clear way to find the underlying review when needed.

## Agree the record and decision owners

Identify the organisation responsible for the prescribed check and the team that maintains its evidence. Use the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for the working arrangement. A change of site does not automatically transfer a statutory excuse to a different legal entity.

Record the worker, role, supplying organisation and applicable sites or assignment. Keep a named reviewer for each requirement and a process for communicating changes to the managers who allocate work.

## Share the relevant outcome

The site manager may need to know that an assignment is authorised, which duties are restricted and whether an action is outstanding. They do not necessarily need access to passports, immigration history or criminal-record detail.

Apply access controls to the source evidence while making the next decision visible. A central system is an operational choice, not a universal legal requirement. Controlled records in another format can also support a reliable process.

## Reconcile movement and exceptions

When a person changes role, site or employing organisation, review the requirements affected. Confirm substitutes against the revised booking. Compare attendance records with expected assignments and investigate differences rather than interpreting a scan as a completed statutory check.

Give each missing result an owner and next action. Missing files are a records problem to resolve; they are not automatically evidence that workers are illegal or that each site owes a penalty.
