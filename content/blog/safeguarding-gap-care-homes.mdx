A safeguarding handoff should explain the person, role and decision. “The agency sent them” is too little information for a manager assigning duties; a broad identity badge is too little information to explain suitability.

## Allocate the decisions explicitly

Set out the agency's evidence responsibilities and the care provider's placement review. For providers in England, use [CQC Regulation 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-19) to assess the role requirements. Professional registration, relevant experience, criminal-record evidence and immigration permission are distinct matters.

For each requirement, identify the source and reviewer. Do not treat a certificate upload as a decision about a disclosure. The appropriate manager needs an outcome and any restrictions, while detailed sensitive evidence should remain access controlled.

## Review changed assignments

Repeat placements can have a different role, location or level of responsibility. Confirm that the decision still covers the assignment. If a replacement worker arrives, stop and reconcile the new worker and evidence with the authorised booking.

A QR attendance entry can help link a person to a recorded arrival. It does not itself carry out a DBS check, confirm professional registration or establish a statutory right-to-work excuse. Keep those processes separate and explain any remaining action.

## Use accurate dates and escalation routes

[DBS certificates do not have an official expiry date](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/dbs-check-requests-guidance-for-employers). Record the issue date and the service's lawful review policy. Avoid declaring someone unsafe solely because an arbitrary renewal interval has elapsed, while still addressing the evidence required by the role.

For a concern about a worker or resident, use the service's safeguarding procedure and relevant reporting route. A dashboard notification is not the response. Keep the action, owner and outcome in the record.
