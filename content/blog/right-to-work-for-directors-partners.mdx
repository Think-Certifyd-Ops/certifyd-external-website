A company title does not tell you the full legal working arrangement. A director may hold an office and also work under another agreement; a partner's situation can differ again. Review the facts before deciding the right-to-work responsibility.

## Start with the agreement and actual work

Read the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) alongside the person's contract or other arrangement. Identify who receives the work, how it is organised and which part of the applicable scheme covers it. The October 2026 guidance addresses specified arrangements beyond a direct contractual employer.

Do not use “paid director”, a PAYE entry or “self-employed partner” as a universal test. Tax treatment, company office and immigration permission answer different questions. A complicated arrangement may need employment and immigration advice.

## Review permission for the proposed activity

Where immigration conditions apply, check the actual result and restrictions against the activity. Permission to work in one role or for a sponsoring employer is not necessarily permission to run another business. Do not infer unrestricted permission from an incorporation record or Companies House appointment.

Use the eligible prescribed check route for the person's circumstances. Physical biometric residence permits are not a substitute for the required online result. Retain the evidence, check date and recorded review, rather than relying on an assurance from another director.

## Resolve an existing gap honestly

If a person has already started work without the required evidence, establish the current position and seek advice on the past period. A current check cannot retrospectively prove that a pre-start check was completed. Equally, a missing document alone does not establish that the person worked illegally.

Record the decision's basis and the next review where appropriate. Review material changes to the activity or arrangement rather than assuming a historic decision covers everything indefinitely.
