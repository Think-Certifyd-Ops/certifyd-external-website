A useful compliance process changes what people do. If a worker record is marked complete while nobody has reviewed its restrictions, the status conceals a decision that still needs to happen.

## Ask what “complete” means

Define completion for each requirement. A received document, a prescribed check and an authorised placement are different stages. For right to work, use the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) to identify the process and evidence, rather than creating a commercial definition of a statutory excuse.

Managers should be able to explain who reviewed the result, what work it covers and what happens if the arrangement changes. A colourful report cannot answer those questions without a reliable underlying record.

## Make exceptions easy to raise

Give staff a route to report missing evidence and uncertain decisions. Record the issue, owner and next step. Do not reward people for turning every status green before the review is complete.

Keep historical actions accurate. A later correction should describe what was corrected; it should not imply the original check happened on time. Missing paperwork is a gap to investigate, not automatic proof of illegal working or an invented fine.

## Test the handoff

Choose a sample of actual placements and follow the information from recruitment to the receiving manager. Check whether substitutions and restrictions reached the person assigning work. Ask the manager which decision they would make if the named worker did not arrive.

Use the findings to improve ownership, access and training. Separate sponsor reporting, safeguarding, employment practice and right-to-work duties rather than describing one regulator as responsible for everything.
