Staffing pressure makes a clear evidence handoff more valuable. It does not justify guessing whether a worker is suitable, or describing an attendance record as a completed safeguarding check.

## Read workforce figures in context

[Skills for Care's October 2025 publication](https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/news-and-events/news/latest-social-care-sector-and-workforce-data-published) describes England's adult social care workforce in 2024/25. It reports 111,000 vacant posts and a 7% vacancy rate. Those figures cover the defined adult social care sector, not just care homes and not the entire UK.

Keep the period, geography and measure beside any figure. A vacancy rate, employee turnover rate and share of agency shifts describe different conditions. A national estimate cannot explain the staffing position of a particular home.

## Prepare the next placement's requirements

List the role, expected person, agency or employer and the evidence needed for the assignment. Give the receiving manager a concise placement decision and an escalation route for a substitute or unresolved requirement.

For regulated providers in England, review [CQC Regulation 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-19). Safeguarding, professional registration and right-to-work evidence require separate processes. A DBS certificate has no official expiry date; a locally scheduled review is a policy decision, not an expiry created by DBS.

## Keep the shift handoff usable

Before assigning duties, reconcile the arriving person with the authorised booking. If the placement changes, record the decision and the manager who approved it. Limit sensitive information to those who need it.

Families may reasonably ask about staffing arrangements, but this does not justify broadcasting worker identity documents or promising automatic family notifications. Follow the service's communication and privacy policies.
