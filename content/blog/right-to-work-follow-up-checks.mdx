Record the next check when you complete the first one. A calendar reminder helps only if it points to the right evidence, person and check method.

The [Home Office employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide), updated on 1 October 2026, explains the follow-up rules and the circumstances that can change them. Use it for the worker’s actual situation.

## Decide whether a further check is required

A correctly conducted check establishing a continuous statutory excuse does not normally require immigration follow-up checks. Time-limited evidence requires a further check at the relevant point to maintain that excuse.

The current document lists distinguish permission-based timing from evidence that supports a six-month statutory excuse. Follow the [official checklist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checklist) rather than assigning every record the same renewal interval.

## Record the date and its meaning

Keep separate fields for the information your team is using:

| Record | What the date means |
| --- | --- |
| Identification document | The document’s own validity or expiry |
| Immigration permission or official check result | The relevant permission or check period |
| Organisation review | The date your team plans to inspect the record again |
| Follow-up action | The next task your reviewer needs to complete |

An early internal reminder can give your team time to request information and resolve questions. It is an operational choice, not a replacement for the legal deadline.

## Follow the appropriate check method

Ask for the evidence needed for the prescribed route. Where the person has an eVisa, use the [employer online right-to-work service](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work) and keep the required result. Where the guidance calls for the [Employer Checking Service](https://www.gov.uk/employee-immigration-employment-status), retain its notice and the timing attached to it.

Do not infer a person’s continuing right to work from an uploaded passport, a historic check or a worker’s statement alone. Review the current evidence through the appropriate route.

## Handle pending applications with care

An application made before existing permission expires can affect the position. The employer guide explains the evidence, continuing-excuse provisions and Employer Checking Service steps that may apply.

A pending application does not create a general grace period for every case. Equally, a date passing is not enough on its own to conclude that every person has lost permission. Use the relevant official guidance and obtain advice where the circumstances are unclear.

## Make the next action visible to the team

Keep the work attached to the record:

1. Identify the person and the evidence behind the current status.
2. Record the relevant date and the reason for the follow-up.
3. Assign the next action and reviewer.
4. Request updated information with enough time for your process.
5. Save the new result and review decision.
6. Update the next date and keep the earlier history in context.

The team should be able to distinguish an approaching date, a request for evidence and a completed review. Those are different stages of the work.
