An outsourced service creates several questions: who engages the worker, what evidence each organisation needs and who authorises work at the site. One “supplier verified” label cannot answer all of them.

## Map the actual chain

Identify the contracting organisations, supplying employer or agency and the site receiving the work. Record who selects the person, agrees duties and receives changes. Consult the [current right-to-work guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for the arrangement's scope and responsibilities.

The October 2026 extended-liability provisions concern relevant contractual arrangements entered into from that date; an existing agreement merely continuing is a different situation. Do not assume every customer receiving a service is automatically the worker's statutory checker. Review the specified categories and exclusions, including services for an individual's own use.

## Agree an evidence handoff

For the site, define the named worker, role, authorised dates, necessary qualifications and decision about any unresolved requirement. Identify the responsible party's prescribed check and the confirmation needed by others. An attendance scan does not create a portable statutory excuse for every business in the chain.

Apply appropriate access controls. A customer may need a deployment decision without receiving copies of all immigration or criminal-record information. Set out lawful sharing and retention arrangements.

## Keep modern-slavery reporting distinct

The [transparency-in-supply-chains guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/transparency-in-supply-chains-a-practical-guide) explains the annual statement requirement for qualifying organisations. It is not a universal requirement to run the same verification check on every supply-chain worker, nor proof that exploitation cannot occur.

Define how workers can raise concerns and how the responsible organisation responds. Review actual warning signs and supplier practices rather than relying entirely on document status.
