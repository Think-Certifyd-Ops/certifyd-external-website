Use the version of the Home Office guidance in force for the check you are conducting. The current employer guide was updated on 1 October 2026; a checklist written earlier in the year may use outdated terms or scope.

## Review the applicable working arrangement

The [October employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) explains responsibility across relevant contractual arrangements, including specified situations beyond a direct contractual employer. Review the actual arrangement and guidance before allocating responsibility.

The extended liability applies to relevant arrangements entered into from 1 October 2026. An existing arrangement merely continuing after that date is not the same as entering into a new arrangement. Review the agreement and any later changes against the guidance's scope.

This does not create an automatic rule that every agency placement requires two identical right-to-work checks or incurs two penalties. Our [agency-worker guide](/blog/agency-worker-compliance-who-checks/) explains the distinction between the responsible party's check and a hirer's placement evidence.

## Keep the check method current

The guidance distinguishes manual checks, the online service, eligible digital right-to-work verification and the Employer Checking Service. Use the route available for the person's circumstances, inspect the required result and retain evidence of the employer's action.

A physical biometric residence permit is not an acceptable substitute for an online right-to-work result. British and Irish citizens have their own acceptable-document routes; they are not all expected to produce immigration share codes. Match the method to the evidence rather than requiring one method from everyone.

## Correct older assumptions in the checklist

The higher civil penalty maxima took effect on 13 February 2024, not January 2026. The [penalty guide](/blog/right-to-work-penalties-2026/) explains why a missing record is different from a finding of illegal working.

EUSS settled and pre-settled status do not require repeat employment checks under the current guidance. Other time-limited cases and verification notices need appropriate follow-up rules. The Fair Work Agency's [labour-market remit](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fair-work-agency-enforcement-policy-statement/fair-work-agency-enforcement-statement) is separate from Home Office illegal-working enforcement.

Give each unresolved record an owner and next action. Certifyd's [records view](/platform/records/) supports that review, while the responsible organisation performs the prescribed checks and decisions.
