Health and Care Worker sponsorship depends on the eligible role and employer, not simply on a job being described as “care”. Review the occupation code, employer eligibility and worker's route before promising sponsorship.

## Check the current route conditions

The [Health and Care Worker guide](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa) explains eligibility and directs employers and applicants to role-specific salary requirements. There is no single salary figure that applies to every health and care occupation.

For care workers and senior care workers, the [current Skilled Worker sponsor guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sponsor-a-skilled-worker/workers-and-temporary-workers-sponsor-a-skilled-worker-accessible) explains the closure to new overseas applications from 22 July 2025. Its in-country switching provision for a worker lawfully employed by the sponsor for at least three months requires an application no later than 22 July 2028, subject to the detailed conditions. A separate branch allows qualifying workers last sponsored in the care occupations to extend or change sponsor where the continuous-permission conditions are met. Do not apply the 2028 deadline to every in-country care-worker case.

## Verify the proposed work and permission

Check the actual role, hours, salary and employer against the sponsorship and immigration conditions. A change of employer or occupation may require more than an internal record update. Supplementary work has particular conditions; do not treat a fixed hours limit as the only requirement.

Complete the appropriate [right-to-work process](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) and retain the employer result. A start date should reflect both permission and the prescribed checking requirements, rather than a generic promise that onboarding can finish within 24 hours.

## Maintain the sponsor evidence

Keep the documents required by [Appendix D](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/keep-records-for-sponsorship-appendix-d). Follow [Part 3](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/workers-and-temporary-workers-guidance-for-sponsors-part-3-sponsor-duties-and-compliance/workers-and-temporary-workers-guidance-for-sponsors-part-3-sponsor-duties-and-compliance-accessible) for reporting events and deadlines. Link recruitment, payroll, absence and work-location changes to the person responsible for sponsorship administration.

Professional registration, DBS eligibility and the provider's staffing suitability decisions remain separate. A sponsor licence alone is not evidence that all care-sector requirements have been met.
