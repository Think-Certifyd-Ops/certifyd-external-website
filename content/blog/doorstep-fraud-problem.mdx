An unexpected visit can create pressure to decide quickly. Before giving access, check whether the appointment and person are expected through a contact route you trust.

## Confirm through the original provider

Use the number or account you used to book the service, or independently find the organisation's official contact details. Do not rely solely on a number, QR code or website offered by the visitor as proof that they represent that organisation.

Ask about a changed worker or appointment. A genuine service provider should be able to explain its authorised handoff. If the visit remains uncertain, keep the door closed while you check and avoid sharing personal or payment information.

## Treat digital messages as another source to review

A message confirming a visit can also be fraudulent. The [NCSC phishing guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/spot-scams) explains precautions for suspicious communications and how to report them. Use independent contact details rather than links in an unexpected urgent message.

Do not treat a familiar voice, logo or professional-looking identity image as proof on its own. Equally, avoid assuming every unexpected caller is fraudulent before checking the facts.

## Make a practical plan

Keep appointment details and the provider's established contact route accessible. If someone needs support with the decision, agree in advance whom they can call. Avoid exposing sensitive worker documents or personal information as a reassurance technique.

If there is immediate danger, use the emergency services. For a suspected fraud or loss, follow the relevant bank and official reporting process promptly; a website record update is not the response.
