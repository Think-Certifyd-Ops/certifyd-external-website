This article is part of Certifyd’s historical security editorial archive. It was revised in October 2026 to remove unsupported claims and earlier product promises. It is not a specification of currently available Certifyd features.


## Define both parties and the action

Mutual authentication means each side verifies a defined claim about the other within a particular system. The strength depends on its evidence, implementation and threat model; it does not prove every later request is authorised.

The [NCSC phishing guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/phishing) explains why layered controls and response remain necessary. A screenshot, compromised account or substituted contact route needs assessment within the actual process.

## Avoid universal promises

This archive does not substantiate the earlier claim that screenshots or impersonation are impossible in a Certifyd mutual-verification system. A design description is not proof that the system is available or independently evaluated.

Certifyd currently organises [worker evidence and reviews](/platform/records/). It does not offer universal reciprocal authentication across every business or household interaction.
