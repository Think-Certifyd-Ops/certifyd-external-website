Seasonal recruitment needs a plan for the sponsored role, arriving worker and evidence handoff. A recruitment booking is not proof that the person can undertake any agricultural work for any employer.

## Check the current route

The [Seasonal Worker visa guide](https://www.gov.uk/seasonal-worker-visa) distinguishes horticulture, for up to six months, from poultry work in the specified annual period. It requires sponsorship and limits the work to the job described in the certificate. Second jobs and permanent jobs are not permitted under this visa.

Use the current route and sponsor guidance for recruitment dates, authorised operators and conditions. Do not rely on a previous year's quota or operator list as though it were current permission.

## Connect permission to the actual assignment

Identify the sponsoring organisation, host business and planned role. Complete the appropriate [right-to-work process](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) and inspect the conditions against the work. Retain the result and relevant dates; an arrival log alone is not a prescribed check.

Agree how a changed location, assignment or worker is reviewed. Give the site manager the decision and restrictions needed for the shift, with access to an appropriate escalation contact.

## Keep pay and welfare reviews separate

Check wage, deduction and accommodation arrangements against the applicable rules. A National Minimum Wage accommodation offset is not a universal ceiling on every housing charge or an immigration fee rule. Review the actual terms and deductions, rather than merging different frameworks into one number.

Make it possible for workers to raise concerns and know how the business responds. A complete set of identity documents cannot establish that employment or accommodation conditions are fair.
