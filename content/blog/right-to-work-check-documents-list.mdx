Start with the check method. The evidence you need depends on the route available to the worker, not simply on whether they have uploaded an identity document.

The Home Office’s [employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) and [checklist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checklist) were updated on 1 October 2026. Use those sources for the complete requirements and any later changes.

## Choose the appropriate route

| Situation | Starting point | What your team needs to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The person can present eligible documents for a manual check | Use the current official document lists and inspect the original evidence | A clear check record and the required copies |
| The person has an eVisa | Use the Home Office employer online service with the share code and date of birth | The employer service result and date of the check |
| Your organisation uses a registered right-to-work digital verification service provider | Confirm the provider and evidence are eligible for the prescribed route | The provider output and your required matching/check record |
| The usual evidence is unavailable or a relevant application is pending | Follow the official Employer Checking Service guidance | The notice and the next review date where applicable |

The worker’s own view of their status and a saved document in an app are useful starting information. They do not replace the employer’s prescribed check.

## Check the document combination

For the manual route, examples in the current [official checklist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checklist/employers-right-to-work-checklist-accessible-version) include:

- A qualifying British passport, current or expired.
- A qualifying Irish passport or passport card, current or expired.
- A UK birth or adoption certificate, long or short, together with the required official document showing the person’s name and permanent National Insurance number.
- A certificate of registration or naturalisation as a British citizen, together with the required official name and National Insurance evidence.

These are examples, not the complete manual document lists. Check the exact combination, document validity and the conditions in the current guidance. An expired document and a cancelled document are different.

## Use the employer’s service for an online check

Where the person has an eVisa, follow the [Home Office employer service](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work). Review the result against the person and the work you are offering, then retain the required output. A photograph of an old residence card is not a substitute for the prescribed online route.

Use the [Employer Checking Service](https://www.gov.uk/employee-immigration-employment-status) where the guidance requires it. Keep the notice with the worker’s record so the next reviewer can see the basis and timing of the check.

## Keep the evidence and decision together

A useful internal record makes the next review easier. Keep a clear connection between:

- The person and the work or assignment being reviewed.
- The method your team used.
- The evidence or official service result.
- The check date and the reviewer.
- Any conditions your team needs to observe.
- The next follow-up date, where one is required.

Name the next action separately from the document status. “Evidence received” should not be mistaken for “check completed”. If evidence is missing or the result needs clarification, the record should show who is following it up.

## Plan the next review

Time-limited permission can require a further check. Read the [follow-up checks guide](/blog/right-to-work-follow-up-checks/) and use the current Home Office rules for the person’s circumstances.

Keep credential expiry, immigration permission and your organisation’s review dates distinct. The expiry printed on an identification document is not always the date on which a person’s permission to work ends.
