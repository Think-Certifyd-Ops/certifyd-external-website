A short assignment still needs a clear decision about the person and work. The useful operational question is whether the arriving worker matches the authorised placement and its evidence requirements.

## Define the check responsibility

Use the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) and actual agreement to identify responsibility. Temporary or supplied work is not outside the scheme merely because a shift is brief. It also does not automatically require both agency and hirer to duplicate every check.

Confirm the prescribed check and the evidence or assurance needed for the receiving organisation. Read our [agency-worker guide](/blog/agency-worker-compliance-who-checks/) before treating a commercial placement checklist as a statutory rule.

## Connect the booking to the worker

Prepare the worker's name, role, location and dates, plus relevant restrictions and role requirements. Give the receiving manager the decision needed to allocate duties. Avoid forwarding a large unreviewed document bundle as a substitute for that decision.

Plan a specific response to substitutions. When a different person arrives, reconcile the changed booking and relevant evidence before assigning the work. Attendance recording can support this process, but a QR entry is not itself a right-to-work or safeguarding check.

## Review the gaps separately

Distinguish “result reviewed”, “evidence missing” and “decision required”. A missing file alone does not establish illegal working, and a ticked status does not establish that a prescribed check happened. Record the reviewer, date and next action.

Use actual staffing volumes and measured administrative effort for any cost comparison. Unsupported national counts or invented per-placement penalty totals are a poor basis for deciding how to improve a process.
