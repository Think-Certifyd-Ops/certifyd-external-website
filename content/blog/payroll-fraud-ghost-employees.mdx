A payroll review asks whether the payment corresponds to authorised employment and the work or entitlement recorded. An identity document alone cannot show that a wage entry, bank change or overtime claim is correct.

## Reconcile the source records

Compare the payroll list with the authorised worker roster, starters, leavers and relevant attendance or entitlement records. Identify duplicate entries, unexplained changes and records without an owner. Treat them as exceptions to review rather than automatically labelling someone a fraudulent “ghost employee”.

Keep right-to-work evidence separate. The [employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) governs that prescribed process; completing it does not certify every subsequent payroll payment or approve additional hours.

## Control sensitive changes

Define who may request, review and authorise payment-detail changes. Verify an unexpected request through an established independent contact route. Do not rely solely on an urgent email, voice message or document attached to the request.

Keep the approved change and reviewer with the record. Restrict access to payment details and avoid collecting extra identity documents when another controlled check is more appropriate.

## Investigate differences fairly

An attendance gap may reflect leave, an incorrect schedule or a recording problem. A matching attendance entry does not prove the payment amount is correct. Use the relevant payroll and employment evidence and involve the authorised manager.

Preserve source records and document the outcome. Where misconduct is suspected, follow the organisation's investigation and reporting procedures rather than turning a software status into an allegation.
