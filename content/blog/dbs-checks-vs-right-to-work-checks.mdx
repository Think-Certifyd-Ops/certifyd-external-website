A DBS certificate and a right-to-work result answer different questions. Neither replaces the other, and neither proves that a worker is suitable for every placement.

## Define the role before requesting a DBS check

[DBS employer guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/dbs-check-requests-guidance-for-employers) explains check levels and eligibility. An enhanced check or barred-list check is available only where the role meets the relevant conditions. Do not request the highest level by default or treat a certificate as a universal pass or fail.

A DBS certificate has no official expiry date. It reflects information at the time of issue. Record its issue date and the organisation's lawful review policy, rather than calling a three-year-old certificate “expired”. Where the Update Service can be used, check the necessary consent and compatibility requirements.

## Review work permission separately

Follow the [Home Office employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for the person's circumstances. Retain the employer's prescribed result, date and review requirements. A physical biometric residence permit, National Insurance number or DBS certificate is not a substitute for the appropriate right-to-work result.

For agency staff, clarify which party conducts the statutory check and which placement evidence the receiving organisation needs. There is no general rule that every hirer must duplicate every agency check. Safeguarding and professional-registration requirements need their own assessment.

## Keep separate decisions visible

An effective worker record distinguishes the check type, source, date, reviewer, relevant restriction and next action. An unresolved DBS disclosure requires the appropriate suitability decision; an immigration restriction requires a review of the actual work.

Control access to criminal-record information and immigration evidence. Give a scheduler the decision they need without exposing unnecessary sensitive detail. A combined green badge can hide the reason a placement needs further review.
