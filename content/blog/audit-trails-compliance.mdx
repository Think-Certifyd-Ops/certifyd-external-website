A useful audit trail explains what happened and why. “Document uploaded” and “check completed” are different events, and a reviewer needs to see which one the record describes.

## Connect the source to the decision

For a prescribed right-to-work check, retain the evidence required by the [employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide), including the relevant result and date. Add the reviewer and any work restrictions or follow-up action. A generic identity match does not replace the prescribed employer process.

For a role requirement, keep the source, issue date and assessment made. A certificate's presence alone cannot explain a suitability decision, particularly where a disclosure or limitation needs review.

## Describe changes accurately

Keep the distinction between an original check, a later correction and a newly received document. Do not backdate a status or silently overwrite evidence to suggest a historical action occurred. Access controls and a change record can support accountability without proving that every entry is correct.

Avoid calling a record “immutable” unless the actual technical and administrative controls justify it. Even an unchanged log can faithfully record a mistaken action. Review the substance of the evidence as well as its history.

## Give an exception an owner

An unresolved record should say what is missing, who must act and what the next step is. A manager reviewing five incomplete files cannot simply multiply five by the maximum illegal-working penalty: missing evidence is not itself a finding of illegal working.

Keep the relevant framework clear. The Fair Work Agency's [labour-market remit](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fair-work-agency-enforcement-policy-statement/fair-work-agency-enforcement-statement) is separate from Home Office illegal-working enforcement and sector-specific safeguarding regulation.
