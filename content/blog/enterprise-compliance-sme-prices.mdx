A small business needs a clear records workflow and a price for the scope it will use. A market-wide claim about what “verification should cost” is less useful than a comparison of actual tasks and current quotes.

## Define the job before comparing prices

List the worker records, role requirements, review dates and attendance handoffs you need to manage. Distinguish that work from prescribed right-to-work checks, safeguarding decisions and professional-registration checks.

Use the [Home Office employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) to identify the statutory process. A records tool, generic identity provider and eligible right-to-work verification service can perform different jobs. Comparing them solely by “price per check” can conceal a scope mismatch.

## Ask for a quote with the assumptions

Confirm worker volume, users, sites, implementation, imports, support and any separate services. Check recurring charges, usage limits, contract length and what happens when records are exported or the service ends.

Use a current written quote rather than an old article for Certifyd pricing. This guide does not state an unverified public price, annual commitment or availability promise. If the offer is a service pilot, confirm its human review and scope separately from software features.

## Test the workflow before accepting the forecast

Walk through receiving evidence, assigning a reviewer and resolving a gap. Ask how your team sees restrictions, upcoming dates and missing decisions. Check access to sensitive files and the handoff when a manager or worker changes.

Use actual administrative effort to estimate value. Do not multiply unchecked records by a maximum penalty or assume installing a tool eliminates enforcement risk.
