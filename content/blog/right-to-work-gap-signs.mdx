A checking gap is a reason to investigate the record. It does not automatically mean a person lacks permission or that a penalty is due. These five signs help identify where the process needs a review.

## The check method is unclear

A file contains a passport copy but no explanation of the prescribed process. Use the [Home Office guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) to establish the route, required employer actions and retained result. Do not infer a completed check from an upload alone.

## The dates do not explain the action

A system shows when a document was uploaded, but not when the employer checked it. Record the actual check date and reviewer. A later upload cannot retrospectively demonstrate a pre-start check.

## Nobody has reviewed job restrictions

The record says “visa received” without connecting its conditions to the proposed work or hours. Review the employer result against the assignment and give the scheduler the relevant decision. Avoid sharing unnecessary sensitive detail.

## The follow-up is a generic annual reminder

Use the applicable rules for time-limited permission, pending applications and verification notices. EUSS settled and pre-settled status do not need repeat employment checks under current guidance. Our [follow-up guide](/blog/right-to-work-follow-up-checks/) explains the distinctions.

## An exception has no owner

A missing item appears in several inboxes but nobody knows who must resolve it. Assign a responsible reviewer, next action and date. Keep the evidence and outcome together, including where the agency or another organisation owns the prescribed check.

A spreadsheet can support a reliable process if it is accurate and controlled. A new dashboard can reproduce the same gaps if status labels have no evidence behind them.
