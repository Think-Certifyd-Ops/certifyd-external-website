Construction sites bring together employers, subcontractors and workers with different roles. A gate record should connect the arriving person to an authorised assignment, while the underlying competence and employment checks remain clear.

## Review the arrangements behind the workforce

Identify who engages each worker and how the relevant contract operates. The [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) addresses specified contractual responsibility; “whoever pays is liable” is too broad to use as a rule.

The October 2026 extended-liability provisions apply to relevant contractual arrangements entered into from 1 October. Review a new or changed agreement against the guidance. Do not assume a principal contractor inherits a transferable statutory excuse merely by viewing a subcontractor's profile.

## Define the role requirements

List the competence, training, safety and access requirements for the actual task and site. Where a card or qualification is relevant, record its source and the review made. A skills card, identity document and right-to-work result answer different questions.

Allocate an owner for each decision. A gate team needs an authorisation outcome and escalation route, rather than unrestricted access to every sensitive employment document.

## Handle substitutions and changed duties

Compare the arriving person with the planned worker and assignment. Where a different individual or subcontractor arrives, review the changed authorisation before assigning duties. An attendance scan is a record of an interaction, not a statutory immigration or competence check.

Keep discrepancies, reviewer and next action together. Review ongoing changes in role or work restrictions with the responsible employer or organisation. Avoid describing a missing record as proof that a person is working illegally.
