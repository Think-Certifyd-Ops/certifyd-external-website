Cleaning work often happens outside normal office hours. Before the first shift, the site needs a practical handoff: the expected worker, permitted areas and the decision behind any role requirements.

## Set responsibilities in the service arrangement

Identify the supplying organisation and who employs or engages the worker. Use the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) to assess the relevant contractual arrangement. Paying a cleaning invoice does not, by itself, decide every party's right-to-work responsibility.

Agree which checks the supplier conducts and what evidence or confirmation the site receives. Keep immigration checks separate from site-security, safety and any role-specific safeguarding requirements. The Fair Work Agency's labour-market remit does not turn a site-access record into a Home Office check.

## Prepare the access handoff

Record the named worker, site, shift and authorised access. Give the receiving manager a contact for changes and a decision about outstanding requirements. Restrict sensitive documents to the appropriate reviewer.

If the supplier substitutes another worker, reconcile that person and the revised authorisation before issuing access. Do not assume a company-level onboarding decision covers every future individual. Review a change in duties where it creates a different requirement.

## Reconcile the attendance record

Compare recorded arrivals with the authorised schedule and investigate differences. A shared worker profile or attendance entry can make that review easier; it does not prove the person completed every employment or safeguarding check, or performed the agreed work.

Keep exceptions with an owner and next step. Where a manager cannot find a result, treat that as a record gap to resolve, rather than automatically accusing the worker of lacking permission.
