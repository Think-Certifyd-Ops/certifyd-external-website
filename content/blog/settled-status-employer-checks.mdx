Employers need to distinguish an initial right-to-work check from a repeat check during employment. That distinction matters for both settled and pre-settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme.

## Use the appropriate initial check

For a new worker relying on digital immigration status, use the employer-facing [Home Office online service](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work). Inspect the result, confirm it relates to the person and retain the required evidence. Asking to see the worker’s personal account or simply collecting a share code does not complete that process.

Read the permissions and restrictions shown by the service. Follow the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for an outstanding EUSS application or other circumstance where the appropriate evidence differs.

Irish citizens can demonstrate their right to work through the eligible evidence described in that guide. They do not need EUSS status to work in the UK. Apply a consistent process without assuming nationality from a name, accent or appearance.

## Do not schedule unnecessary repeat EUSS checks

The 1 October 2026 employer guide states that employment right-to-work checks do not need to be repeated for holders of either settled or pre-settled status. Earlier advice that every pre-settled-status worker required a repeat check at the displayed expiry date is out of date.

Review reminders that were created under that earlier process. Distinguish an obsolete immigration recheck from a separate, justified company review, such as checking a professional registration or training requirement. Record why a reminder was changed so another reviewer does not recreate it.

## Treat historical employment records carefully

The guide describes the position for EEA citizens employed before 1 July 2021. Do not launch a blanket retrospective nationality-based checking exercise. A valid historical check is not made defective simply because the current document list or digital service has changed.

If you discover a missing check or information that calls a worker’s permission into question, use the guidance applicable to the actual circumstances. Missing paperwork alone does not prove illegal working. Decisions affecting the worker need a fair process and, where necessary, individual advice.

## Keep the evidence useful to the next reviewer

A worker record should explain the check method, date and outcome, identify the retained evidence and record who reviewed it. Avoid a vague status such as “visa checked” when the underlying result establishes something more specific.

Protect access to immigration evidence and keep it for the prescribed period. The employment team should be able to retrieve the result without giving everyone unrestricted access to the person’s documents.
