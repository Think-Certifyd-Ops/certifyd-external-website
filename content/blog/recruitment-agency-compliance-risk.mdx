Recruitment agencies manage information between candidates, employers and hirers. The risk is often a handoff that nobody owns: a changed worker, an unclear restriction or a check result that cannot be found.

## Identify the working arrangement

Read the agreement and actual work alongside the [Home Office employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide). Responsibility depends on the applicable arrangement. Do not assume every placement creates automatic joint illegal-working liability for agency and hirer, or that every hirer must repeat a standard agency check.

Document who conducts the prescribed process and which confirmation the receiving organisation needs. See [who checks agency workers](/blog/agency-worker-compliance-who-checks/) for the distinction between employer checks and placement evidence.

## Send a placement decision with its basis

Link the named worker to the role, location and dates. Record relevant job restrictions, qualifications, registration or safeguarding decisions. A CV and an onboarding-complete flag cannot explain whether a particular assignment is appropriate.

Agree how the hirer receives changes. If the booked person is replaced, check the substitute's requirements before confirming the placement. Do not copy the original worker's status to a replacement or use one generic agency-level badge as evidence for everyone.

## Make exceptions actionable

Record missing evidence, the person responsible and the deadline for the next action. Keep a completed check's actual result and date. Separate unresolved permission from a document-retrieval problem; the latter alone is not a finding of illegal working.

Maintain access controls so the hirer receives the placement decision and necessary evidence without unrestricted access to sensitive documents. Contractual information sharing needs its own lawful basis and scope.
