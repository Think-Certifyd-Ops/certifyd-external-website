A concern about workforce checks or employment practices needs an owner and a response. Suppressing the report or quietly changing a record can make the underlying problem harder to understand.

## Define the route for raising concerns

The [government whistleblowing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/whistleblowing) explains qualifying disclosures and the public-interest requirement. Not every complaint is automatically a protected disclosure. Personal grievances and wider wrongdoing can require different processes, and a particular case may need advice.

Use the [employer guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/whistleblowing-guidance-for-employers) to review the organisation's arrangements. Tell workers whom to contact, how an alternative route operates and what happens after a report. Avoid promising absolute anonymity or confidentiality if the actual process cannot guarantee it.

## Preserve the facts and review the risk

Record the concern, relevant source evidence and the person responsible for assessing it. Restrict sensitive information and avoid adverse assumptions about the reporter. Where there is an urgent safeguarding or safety issue, use the appropriate immediate response rather than waiting for a routine records review.

For alleged right-to-work failures, separate missing evidence from a finding of illegal working. Use the [Home Office guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for the prescribed process and retain an honest record of corrections.

## Document the response

Keep actions, owner and outcome together. Consider whether a procedural change, training or specialist review is needed. Report back within the organisation's lawful process without disclosing unnecessary personal information.

The Fair Work Agency's [labour-market remit](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fair-work-agency-enforcement-policy-statement/fair-work-agency-enforcement-statement) is distinct from Home Office immigration enforcement. The relevant reporting body depends on the issue, not a generic “compliance” label.
