An unsuccessful hire can lead to recruitment rework, disrupted schedules and additional management time. The cost depends on the role and circumstances; a universal salary multiplier can obscure more than it explains.

## List the actual costs

Record advertising, agency charges, interviewing, onboarding, training and replacement effort. Include manager time and any temporary cover the organisation actually purchases. Separate cash expenditure from an estimate of internal time.

Define the period and avoid double counting. A replacement cost and the original recruitment cost may belong to different questions. Label estimates where the business has no measured figure.

## Review the evidence handoff

Check whether the hiring manager had the role requirements, relevant qualifications and completed decisions before assigning work. If a handoff failed, identify the information and owner that would improve it next time.

Prescribed right-to-work checks follow the [Home Office guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide). Missing evidence does not automatically mean a person worked illegally, and multiplying incomplete files by maximum civil penalties is not a measured hiring cost. Actual notices and legal issues need their own assessment.

## Improve a defined part of the process

Choose an observable action, such as reducing duplicate document requests or making unresolved role requirements visible to the scheduler. Measure a small set of cases before and after the change. Include exceptions and implementation effort.

Do not promise to eliminate bad hires through identity verification. A genuine identity and permission to work do not establish performance, suitability for every role or future conduct. Recruitment judgement and sector-specific decisions remain necessary.
