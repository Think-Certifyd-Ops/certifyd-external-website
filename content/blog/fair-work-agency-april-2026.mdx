The Fair Work Agency launched on 7 April 2026. Employers should now use its published enforcement guidance, rather than a forecast about a future April launch.

Its purpose concerns labour-market enforcement. It is not a replacement name for the Home Office’s immigration enforcement service.

## Identify the duty and the responsible body

Start with the [Fair Work Agency enforcement statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fair-work-agency-enforcement-policy-statement/fair-work-agency-enforcement-statement). Its functions and territorial coverage depend on the relevant legislation and implementation timetable.

For your organisation, identify the applicable duties concerning pay, employment agencies, labour provision or other covered employment practices. Keep the source guidance beside the owner responsible for reviewing each duty.

Do not apply illegal-working civil penalty figures to every missing employment record. The amounts and enforcement process depend on the actual breach and the legislation involved.

## Follow employment records back to the work performed

For pay and hours, check whether the records explain what the person did, the hours and payments involved, and the basis used for the calculation. Resolve discrepancies between payroll records, schedules and the underlying work.

For an agency or labour-supply arrangement, identify the parties and their responsibilities. Keep the agreement, relevant worker records and evidence of the checks or licensing duties that apply to that arrangement.

A tidy folder is useful only if it contains the evidence needed to understand the actual practice. Assign a reviewer and record the action taken when information is missing or inconsistent.

## Keep immigration checks in their own process

The Home Office employer guidance governs prescribed right-to-work checks and statutory excuses against illegal-working civil penalties. The [October 2026 guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) also addresses the scope of direct and non-direct contractual arrangements.

Those rules do not mean every hirer duplicates an agency employer’s checks, or that the Fair Work Agency automatically fines an employer for each incomplete immigration file. Review the real arrangement and the appropriate guidance.

## Prepare a practical records review

Create a list of the employment processes your organisation needs to demonstrate. For each one, record the applicable source, evidence location, reviewer, retention requirement and next action.

Prioritise unanswered pay or worker-status questions, unclear agency responsibilities and records that another authorised reviewer cannot retrieve. Keep an action log that explains how the problem was resolved.
