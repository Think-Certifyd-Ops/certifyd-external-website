A small team benefits from a short, accurate checking process. Review these gaps before adding more reminders or buying a tool to automate a process nobody has defined.

## Treat the evidence as a process

An emailed passport image is not a complete manual check. Use the [current employer guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide) for the eligible route, employer actions and retained result. A National Insurance number or bank account is not a substitute for work-permission evidence.

For an online check, retain the employer result and compare it with the person and actual job. Do not rely on the worker's own status screenshot or a physical biometric residence permit.

## Avoid blanket document rules

An expired British or Irish passport can be acceptable for the relevant manual route. Digital-provider eligibility and other evidence routes have different conditions. “Every document must be current” is not an accurate replacement for the prescribed lists.

Apply a consistent policy while allowing the acceptable routes. Do not ask only workers with a particular accent or appearance, or insist that every British citizen obtains an immigration share code.

## Set the right follow-up

Use the [follow-up guide](/blog/right-to-work-follow-up-checks/) and official requirements. EUSS settled and pre-settled status do not require repeat employment checks. Some pending applications and verification notices have specific rules; an old visa date does not settle every case.

## Make a gap actionable

Record the missing result, reviewer and next step. A current check cannot prove that an earlier pre-start check occurred. A missing document also does not, by itself, prove illegal working or an automatic penalty.
