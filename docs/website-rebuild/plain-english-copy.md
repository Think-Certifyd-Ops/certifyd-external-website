# Plain-English copy (11 November 2026)

Goal: a 12-year-old should understand every headline on first read. Andrew's sample homepage headlines:

- Every worker's documents, collected, checked and kept up to date. **(used as the homepage H1)**
- Hiring paperwork, done for you. **(used as the footer line)**
- We collect and check every document a worker needs to start.
- The documents every worker needs, collected for you.

## Rules we wrote to

1. Use words people say out loud. "Job", not "role". "Worker", not "operative". "Up to date", not "current" or "compliant".
2. Say what happens, not what it's called. "We chase the missing documents", not "We do the chasing, so your team stays compliant".
3. One idea per headline. If a headline has a comma and an "and", try splitting it.
4. Keep the official names people search for (DBS, right to work, CQC, SIA, CSCS, NHS), but put plain words around them.
5. FAQ headings all follow one pattern: "Questions [who] ask us."

## The body copy: what makes it hard, and how to fix it

Measured on the built pages (paragraphs and list items, Flesch-Kincaid grade):

| Page | Grade | Sentences over 20 words |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | 5.8 | 5 |
| How it works | 5.3 | 0 |
| For workers | 3.9 | 1 |
| Pricing | 3.7 | 0 |
| Healthcare staffing | 6.2 | 1 |
| Security firms | 5.1 | 2 |
| Regulations hub | 8.5 | 2 |
| Role page (registered nurse) | 8.1 | 4 |
| Right-to-work guide | 8.1 | 1 |
| Right-to-work change, Oct 2026 | 9.0 | 1 |

The sentences are mostly short already. What makes the copy hard is **words that name a concept instead of saying what happens**. A grade score doesn't catch those, so they're the main thing to fix.

1. **Swap trade words for everyday ones.** These come up most often:

   | Instead of | Say |
   |---|---|
   | compliance, compliant | following the rules; checks that are up to date; cleared to work |
   | onboard, onboarding | take on, start, sign up |
   | exceptions | workers who need you; anything missing or wrong |
   | evidence pack | a file of proof; the worker's file |
   | audit-ready | ready when someone asks to see it |
   | expiry, expiry date | runs out; renewal date |
   | template | ready-made checklist |
   | screening, vetting | background checks |
   | operative | worker |
   | deploy | send |
   | credential | licence, card or certificate |
   | retrospective | only counts from (date) |
   | liability | who can be fined |

2. **Put plain words next to official names the first time they appear.** Keep the names, because buyers search for them, but add a few words: "a DBS check (a criminal record check)", "CQC, the care regulator", "an SIA licence (the licence security officers need)", "a share code (a code from GOV.UK that proves someone can work here)".

3. **Start every page with the payoff in one or two sentences.** The regulations pages are the hardest to read (grade 8 to 9) because they open with the law. Add a short "In short" box at the top of each guide and role page: what the reader must do, by when, and what happens if they don't. Keep the legal detail underneath for people who want it.

4. **Split long sentences.** There are 5 on the homepage and 4 on each role page. Most are a list joined with "and" or "so". Cut them into two sentences.

5. **Use "we" and "you".** "Certifyd collects..." becomes "We collect...". "Workers can..." becomes "You can..." on the workers page. People find it easier to follow who does what.

6. **Use the same word for the same thing everywhere.** The site says "starter", "new starter", "worker", "operative", "officer" and "temp" for one idea. Use "worker" in general copy and the trade word ("nurse", "officer") only on that sector's page. It's the same with "record", "file" and "evidence pack": pick "file" for what the customer downloads.

7. **Make the pictures say the same thing.** The product mock-ups use "on the record", "exceptions" and "evidence pack". When the copy changes, update the labels in the illustrations so the words match.

## Body copy pass (done 11 November 2026)

Rules 1 to 7 were applied to the body copy of every new-design page: homepage, How it works, For workers, Pricing, Who it's for, the eight sector pages, the regulations hub, the five guides and the role template (all 150 role pages).

What changed:

- **Trade words swapped.** "Evidence pack" is now "worker file", "exceptions" is "needs you", "expiry" is "runs out" or "end date", "onboard" is "take on" or "sign up", "screening" is "background checks", "operative" is "worker", "template" is "checklist", "civil penalty" is "fine", and "role" is "job" in reader-facing copy.
- **Official names explained.** DBS (criminal record) check, CQC (the care regulator), COSHH (cleaning chemicals), share code (a GOV.UK code that proves you can work here), Regulation 19, the Conduct Regulations, statutory excuse, "regulated activity" and the designated premises supervisor all get a few plain words the first time they appear. Role pages spell out the official body, for example "NMC (Nursing and Midwifery Council)".
- **"In short" box** at the top of every role page and every guide: what to do, in two or three sentences. On role pages it's built from the checklist, for example: "Before a practice nurse starts, check their ID and their right to work in the UK. Then check NMC register and revalidation date, enhanced DBS with adults' barred list and 3 years of references. There are 4 more checks on the list below."
- **Long sentences split**, especially on the regulations pages. The "who can be fined" section is now a short list.
- **One word for one thing.** "Worker" in general copy, the trade word only on its sector page; "file" for what the customer downloads; "cleared" for a worker who has passed every check (the dashboards said "Verified").
- **Pictures match the words.** Product mock-ups now say "Worker file", "Needs you", "One list", "Checked", "In progress", "Running out" and "Save check-in".
- **Small fixes.** "an HGV driver" and "an SIA officer" on role pages, where it used to say "a HGV".
